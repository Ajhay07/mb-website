Quality Assurance & Quality Control

Every order is designed, manufactured and tested against the standard it was quoted against, not a catalogue approximation of it.

Our quality policy

We’d rather hold a delivery date for a day than ship a unit that doesn’t match its own drawing. That preference is built into how the shop runs: a drawing is checked before it is released, a unit is checked against that drawing before it ships, and the test records exist to prove both happened, not to paper over the gap if they didn’t.

Incoming material inspection

Plate, sheet and bar stock are checked against their mill certificates on arrival, heat number by heat number, before anything is cut. Material that cannot be traced to a certificate does not go into a job, regardless of how closely it otherwise matches specification.

In-process inspection

Dimensional checks happen after forming, after welding and before final assembly, not only at the end of the job. Catching a deviation at the forming stage costs a re-form; catching the same deviation after final assembly costs the unit.

Final inspection & release

A unit is released only once its dimensional check, weld inspection and hydrostatic test are all complete and recorded against the drawing. The certification pack is compiled from those records at that point, not written up separately afterward.

Non-conformance handling

A deviation found at any stage is logged, not worked around. Depending on severity it is corrected, reworked to an approved repair procedure, or the material is rejected outright; what it is never is shipped unrecorded on the assumption it probably will not matter.

Calibration

Pressure gauges, test rigs and measuring equipment used for inspection and testing are calibrated on a fixed schedule against traceable standards, so a hydrostatic test result means the same thing on a unit tested this month as one tested last year.

Documentation that travels with the unit

  • Material test certificates (MTC) traceable to heat number
  • Weld procedure and welder qualification records
  • Dimensional inspection report against the approved drawing
  • NDT reports (dye penetrant and/or radiography as specified)
  • Hydrostatic test certificate to design pressure

Questions we hear first

Can you work to a customer-supplied inspection and test plan (ITP)?

Yes, we work to your ITP where one is supplied, or propose one based on the applicable design code and specification where it is not.

What do you do with a unit that fails final inspection?

It is held, logged as a non-conformance, and either reworked to an approved procedure and re-inspected, or scrapped; it does not ship against an exception.

Is your testing accredited by a third party?

Hydrostatic and NDT testing is performed in-house to the governing code; independent witnessing by agencies such as Lloyd’s or Bureau Veritas is arranged on request for jobs that specify it.
